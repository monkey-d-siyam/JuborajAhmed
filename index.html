About Me

AI Researcher

I am a recent Computer Science and Engineering graduate from BRAC University with research interests in trustworthy artificial intelligence, federated learning, medical AI, vision-language models, and LLM security. My undergraduate thesis investigated a privacy-preserving federated learning framework for remote patient monitoring.

I am also involved in research on trustworthy vision-language models for medical image understanding and geometric defenses against prompt poisoning in federated LoRA systems. I am particularly interested in understanding how AI systems behave under uncertainty, adversarial pressure, privacy constraints, and limited computational resources.

My long-term goal is to pursue doctoral research focused on developing reliable and interpretable AI systems for high-stakes applications.

Investigating reliability challenges in medical vision-language models, including sycophancy, hallucination, calibration, and robustness under misleading or conflicting prompts. The research aims to understand how multimodal models represent and respond to clinically consequential pressure.

Undergraduate Thesis 2025 — 2026

JASAL: A Remote Patient Health Monitoring System Driven by Federated Learning

BRAC University

Developed and evaluated a federated learning framework for physiological anomaly detection using 32,796 patient records distributed across 10 non-IID client partitions. The work investigated differential privacy, secure aggregation, adaptive patient profiling, interpretable anomaly detection, model quantization, and communication-efficient deployment.

  • Evaluated a deep-autoencoder-based anomaly detection framework across non-IID federated clients.
  • Investigated differential privacy and secure multiparty computation against privacy attacks.
  • Optimised the model for edge deployment using INT8 quantisation and communication-efficient training.
Federated Learning Differential Privacy Anomaly Detection PyTorch Edge Deployment
